# Which used or refurbished phone should you buy in 2026?

**Short answer: a Pixel 8 or newer, a Galaxy S24 or S25, or an iPhone 13 or newer.** Those still have roughly **four or more years** of security updates ahead. Skip the **Pixel 6, Pixel 7, Galaxy S21 and S22, and iPhone XR**: their updates have ended or end within about a year, so the "bargain" turns into a phone your bank app eventually refuses. This page ranks popular refurbished phones by how long they'll stay supported, using each maker's own promises where they exist and clearly marked **estimates** where they don't.

## Why updates matter more than specs on a used phone

A phone without security updates still turns on, but new security holes stop getting fixed. Banking, payment and work apps gradually stop supporting old versions. So the real price of a used phone is **price ÷ years of updates left**. A €150 phone with one year left costs more per year than a €300 phone with four.

## Our picks

- **Best value Android: Pixel 8 or Pixel 8a.** Four-plus years of guaranteed updates, and they're the oldest Pixels on the 7-year promise, so they're usually the cheapest way in. Details: [when does the Pixel 8 stop getting updates](../when-does-pixel-8-stop-getting-updates/).
- **Best Samsung: Galaxy S24.** It's getting One UI 9 now and has four more Android upgrades promised. A used [Galaxy S23](../when-does-galaxy-s23-stop-getting-updates/) is cheaper but is on its last major upgrade. If the price gap is small, a used [Galaxy S25](../when-does-galaxy-s25-stop-getting-updates/) adds about a year (to about February 2032, our estimate).
- **Best iPhone on a budget: iPhone 13.** It runs iOS 27, the same version as the newest iPhones. Apple gives no date, so this is a judgement call based on history. If you want something Apple has put in writing, an **iPhone 15** has a minimum until September 2028.

## What about the new EU rules?

From **20 June 2025**, the EU's ecodesign rules for smartphones ([Regulation (EU) 2023/1670](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A32023R1670)) say that makers who update a phone's operating system must keep those updates available, free, for at least **five years after the model stops being sold** in the EU ([European Commission](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/news/new-eu-rules-durable-energy-efficient-and-repairable-smartphones-and-tablets-start-applying-2025-06-20_en)). That's good news for phones launched from mid-2025 on. For the older models on this page, the maker's own promise is the safer thing to plan around, so that's what we used.

## Five checks before you pay

1. **Look up the model's end-of-support date** on our [device list](../../index.html). Not the year you buy it: support counts from the model's launch.
2. **Check it's on a current version**: Android 17 for Pixels, One UI 8.5 or 9 for Galaxys, iOS 26 or 27 for iPhones.
3. **Make sure it's signed out of the previous owner's account** (Google account or Apple ID / Find My). Otherwise it can lock you out after a reset.
4. **Check it isn't carrier-locked or reported stolen.**
5. **Ask about the battery** and the warranty. A refurbished phone from a business seller in the EU comes with a legal guarantee; a private sale usually doesn't.
