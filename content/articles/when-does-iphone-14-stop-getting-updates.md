# When does the iPhone 14 stop getting updates?

**Short answer: not any time soon.** On 19 October 2026 the **iPhone 14, 14 Plus, 14 Pro and 14 Pro Max** are all on Apple's list of iPhones that run **iOS 27**, the newest version, and the latest update is **iOS 27.0.1 (28 September 2026)**. Apple doesn't announce end dates, but it has put one minimum in writing: its UK compliance statements for all four models promise security updates for at least **five years from 16 September 2022**, so until at least **September 2027**. Based on how Apple treated older iPhones, our **estimate** is that the iPhone 14 gets new major iOS versions until 2027 or 2028 (the 14 Pro possibly a year longer), with security fixes until roughly **2030 to 2031**. That's our reading of Apple's history, not a promise from Apple.

Apple's [How to download iOS 27](https://support.apple.com/en-us/128058) page lists the **iPhone 14, iPhone 14 Plus, iPhone 14 Pro and iPhone 14 Pro Max** as compatible. Apple's [security releases page](https://support.apple.com/en-us/100100) says "The latest version of iOS and iPadOS is 27.0.1" and lists **iOS 27** (14 September 2026) and **iOS 27.0.1** (28 September 2026) for "iPhone 11 and later". If you'd rather stay on iOS 26 for now, **iOS 26.7.1** came out the same day for the same phones. So right now the iPhone 14 gets exactly the same updates as the newest iPhones.

Apple Intelligence and the new Siri AI need an **iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone Air or iPhone 16 or newer** ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/121115)), so the iPhone 14 family, Pro models included, gets iOS 27 and its security fixes but not those AI features.

## What Apple has actually promised

Since the UK's product security law (PSTI) took effect on 29 April 2024, Apple has had to publish a minimum support period for phones it sells there. Its statements of compliance for the [iPhone 14 (A2882)](https://regulatoryinfo.apple.com/cwt/api/ext/file?fileId=securityTelecommunication%2FiPhone+14+%28Model+A2882%29_V0.pdf), [iPhone 14 Plus (A2886)](https://regulatoryinfo.apple.com/cwt/api/ext/file?fileId=securityTelecommunication%2FiPhone+14+Plus+%28Model+A2886%29_V0.pdf), [iPhone 14 Pro (A2890)](https://regulatoryinfo.apple.com/cwt/api/ext/file?fileId=securityTelecommunication%2FiPhone+14+Pro+%28Model+A2890%29_V0.pdf) and [iPhone 14 Pro Max (A2894)](https://regulatoryinfo.apple.com/cwt/api/ext/file?fileId=securityTelecommunication%2FiPhone+14+Pro+Max+%28Model+A2894%29_V0.pdf) all say:

- **First supply date:** 16 September 2022
- **Defined support period:** "Minimum 5 Years from the first supply date"

That's a floor, not an end date. Apple has usually gone well past five years (see below), so don't read September 2027 as the day your iPhone 14 stops getting updates.

## How long will it really get updates? (our estimate)

Apple doesn't publish a support period beyond that minimum, so the best guide is what it did before. These dates come from Apple's own pages.

- **iPhone 8 and iPhone X** (2017, both A11 chip): got new major versions up to **iOS 16**. **iOS 17** (2023) dropped them, about **6 years** after launch. Security-only updates kept coming: **iOS 16.7.16 arrived on 11 May 2026**.
- **iPhone XS, XS Max and XR** (2018, all A12): got new major versions up to **iOS 18**. **iOS 26** (2025) dropped them, about **7 years** after launch. **iOS 18.7.10 still arrived on 17 August 2026**.
- **iPhone 11** (2019, A13): still on **iOS 27**, seven years in. See [Is the iPhone 11 still supported?](../is-iphone-11-still-supported/)

Two things stand out. First, Apple tends to drop iPhones **by chip**: the iPhone 8 and X went together, and so did the XS and XR. Second, after dropping a phone it has kept shipping security-only fixes for **two to three years or more**.

What that means for the iPhone 14 (**our estimate**):

- **iPhone 14 and 14 Plus (A15):** they share their chip with the [iPhone 13](../when-does-iphone-13-stop-getting-updates/), which is a year older. If Apple drops by chip again, they could lose new major versions **together with the iPhone 13**, in **September 2027 or September 2028**.
- **iPhone 14 Pro and 14 Pro Max (A16):** they share their chip with the iPhone 15 and 15 Plus, so they may well get **one more major version** than the regular 14.
- **Security fixes after that:** going by the iPhone 8 and XS, roughly **2030 to 2031**.

Treat this as an educated guess. Apple can change course at any time, and iOS 27 surprised everyone by not dropping a single iPhone.

## What happens when updates stop?

- **First, no new iOS features.** The phone stays on its last major version and gets smaller security-only updates for a while.
- **Then the fixes thin out.** Apple doesn't announce when security-only updates for an old version end; they just stop appearing.
- **Apps follow eventually.** Banking, payment and messaging apps start requiring a newer iOS version at some point, not overnight. For an iPhone 14 that's years away.

## What should iPhone 14 owners do?

1. **Install the latest update now:** Settings → General → Software Update. You should see iOS 27.0.1 (or iOS 26.7.1 if you chose to stay on iOS 26).
2. **Keep using it.** An iPhone 14 is fully supported in October 2026, including for banking, DigiD, WhatsApp and work email.
3. **Check the battery** before you think about replacing the phone: Settings → Battery → Battery Health & Charging. A new battery is far cheaper than a new iPhone.
4. **Watch WWDC in June 2027 and 2028.** That's when Apple reveals which iPhones the next iOS supports.

## Should you buy a used iPhone 14 in 2026?

**Yes, at the right price, but compare it with an iPhone 15.** A used iPhone 14 runs the current iOS and should get several more years of updates (our estimate), but Apple's written minimum only runs to **September 2027**. A used **iPhone 15** has a written minimum until **September 2028** ([Apple's UK filing](https://regulatoryinfo.apple.com/cwt/api/ext/file?fileId=securityTelecommunication%2FiPhone+15+%28Model+A3090%29_V0.pdf)) and has USB-C, so if the price gap is small, the 15 is the safer buy. Between the iPhone 14 models, the **14 Pro** has the newer A16 chip, which may buy it an extra year of major updates (our estimate). An **iPhone 13** is usually cheaper but has the same A15 chip as the regular 14 and is a year older.
